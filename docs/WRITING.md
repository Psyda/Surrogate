# Writing

This applies to every word a player reads or hears: dialogue, radio, terminals, books,
signs, objectives, errand text. It also applies to design docs.

## The rules

1. Full sentences. Every line has a subject and a verb. "There's a scrubber on the north
   wall." Never "Scrubber, north wall."
2. People talk like people. Contractions are the default. "I'm", "don't", "you've",
   "that's". A character only drops them when they're being deliberately formal.
3. Don't get clunky and don't get too concise. A line should sound like someone said it
   out loud to a friend. If it sounds like a checklist, a telegram, or a poem, rewrite it.
4. No em-dashes. No en-dashes. No semicolons. Anywhere. Use a full stop or a comma.
5. Say what you mean. If an instruction is in the line, the player should know what to
   do and where by the end of it. Add a word of warmth or a reason when it fits.

## The trap to avoid

The natural failure mode is stripping lines down until only nouns are left. It reads as
cold and confusing and it makes everyone sound like the same robot.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| Scrubber, north wall. Green lamp, you are breathing. Red, the room is open and you have ten seconds. Dark, no power. | There's a scrubber on the north wall. Green light means you're breathing. If it ever goes red, you've got ten seconds to get sealed up. |
| The field radio. Chest, north wall. Hold it and use it. I can hear the carrier from here. | There's a field radio in the chest by the wall. Grab it and talk to me. I can hear you keying. |
| ...I left my clipboard on your table. Keep it. Read the forms. Laugh at the forms. They are not funny. | Oh, I left my clipboard on your table. Keep it. Read the forms when you're bored. They're funnier than they mean to be. |
| He comes up from the south. Watch the glass, or the airlock. | He'll come in from the south. Go stand at the glass, you'll see his lamp before he gets to the door. |
| The chair. Sit in it. | Go ahead and sit in the chair. I'll talk you through it. |
| Card on the chassis, then on the chair. The chair is yours now. Nobody else's card goes in it. | Touch the card to the chassis first, then to the chair. That chair's yours now. Nobody else's card goes in it. |
| Sit. The calibration on the ship does not count. Ten seconds to boot and it feels like a minute. Arms go first. | Sit down. The calibration you did on the ship doesn't count for much. It takes about ten seconds to boot and it'll feel like a minute. Your arms come in first. |
| It is a pad, is what it is. They want to put something up. Get some plate down and we will find out what. | It's a landing pad. They want to build something on it. Lay plating across the middle and we'll find out what. |

Errand text has the same problem in a different coat. "She permits it." and "That is one
back together." are fragments dressed up as style. Write them as sentences too.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| She permits it. | She lets you pick her up, which is more than she does for most people. |
| That is one back together. | That's one of them running again. |
| The cat is out. She has been out for some hours. Bring her back. | Ballast got out a few hours ago. Go find her and bring her home. |

## The cast

| Character | Who they are | How they sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Halloran | Station lead at Site Two. Has been alone a long time and has decided things. | Calm, plain, a little dry. Gives reasons without being asked twice. |
| Marsh | Company representative who fixes everything and is starting to hate the company. | Wry, tired, kind underneath. Jokes about the forms. |
| Sorensen | Mechanic at Survey Two. Glad to have someone to talk to. | Warm, chatty, talks about the machine in front of him. |
| Okafor | Scientist who is done being polite. | Direct. Says the true thing first, then softens it if she feels like it. |
| Castellanos | Pilot who never stays. | Quick, cheerful, always halfway out the door. |
| Ferreira | Doctor. | Gentle, one instruction at a time, never rushed. |
| Teague | Engineer who doesn't look up from the work. | Short answers, but still sentences. Talks to the part, not to you. |

## Examples

These are the target. Match the tone, not the exact words.

### Halloran, first contact

> Site Four, this is Site Two. You're down. Say something so I know the pod didn't land on you.
>
> There you are. I'm Ines Halloran, I run Site Two. The other voice is Marsh. He's the company, but he's alright.
>
> They were supposed to drop you here with us. They dropped you at Four instead. That's six hundred metres of sand between us, so for now you get us as voices. Sorry about that.
>
> Couple of things before you touch anything. The airlock has two doors. Never open both at once. That little chamber is the only reason your pod isn't a coffin.
>
> The roof's glass because somebody at the company wanted a farm out here. It's airtight. Please don't go testing that.

### Halloran, sending the player outside

> That's the wastes. Caustic sand, sulfur, and a sky that never clears. Everything out there wants to dissolve you. The chassis doesn't care, and that's the whole point of it.
>
> Keep an eye on your power. You get about half an hour on a charge, less if you run. When it dies, it dies where it's standing, so be home before that.
>
> One more thing. Your body's in that chair. Whatever happens out there, you're really in here. If the body line ever says breach, you drop everything and you come home.

### Marsh, arriving on foot

> Marsh here. I'm two hours out, on foot, in a suit that's rated for one. I'm coming to fix your dock. Don't open the outer door until you can see me standing in front of it.
>
> Okay, I'm at your door. Outer door, please. And don't tell the company I said please.
>
> Huh. Yours is smaller than ours.
>
> Teo Marsh. Company representative, which means I carry the clipboard. Station fixer, which means I carry everything else.
>
> Your dock's wired for the ship's bus, not the roof's. It charges at a crawl. Hand me a wrench and I'll make it honest.

### Marsh, reading the company line

> This is the company representative reading you the line I have to read. Sallow Assay, Contract Seven. Samples go in the crate, numbers go to the relay. When the assay's complete, the company sends the ship.
>
> Halloran: He has to say that. Nobody's ever seen the ship.
>
> Marsh: I have to say that.

### Sorensen, building the mast

> There you are. Mikkel Sorensen, Survey Two. Good to finally meet you in person. Sort of.
>
> I've got the mast parts on my chassis. Stand back a little and keep an eye on that corner.
>
> Up she goes. Give it a second.
>
> That'll hold. I told you it would hold.
>
> Right, lights next. Stay where you are, I can see you fine from here.

### Okafor, giving the survey

> I need a reading of everything alive on this planet. Every animal, every growth. I've given you the sampler and the software. The rest you'll have to go and find.
>
> Don't bring me guesses. If you're not sure what it is, sample it twice.
>
> That's the table full. Good. Now I get to start arguing with it.

### Ferreira, in the clinic

> Sit down for me. Take a breath. Now another one.
>
> Your body's been in that chair for nine hours. That's too long. Eat something before you go back out.
>
> There's a body about fifteen metres from my airlock. I've been looking at it through the window for nine weeks. My chassis has been dead for eleven. I'd like him buried, if you can.
>
> Not there. I can see that spot from the window. Somewhere with ground to dig and sky over it.

### Teague, at the bench

> Hang on. Nearly got it.
>
> Hand me the small one. No, the other small one.
>
> There. That's holding. Don't lean on it.

### Castellanos, on the radio

> Morning, Site Four. Castellanos. I've got your supplies and I'm not stopping, so get to the pad.
>
> Halloran asked me for a cat. I don't ask questions anymore. There's a cat.
>
> Right, that's me. Tell Ines I said hi. Actually don't, she'll want to talk.

### Errands and objectives

Errand briefs are a narrator, not a character. Still full sentences, still warm.

> Sorensen has been eating out of foil packets for fourteen months. Cook him something and get it to him while it's still warm.
>
> He didn't say anything for a while. Then he asked if there was any more.
>
> Three of Brandt's neighbours have had their machines eaten by the rain. He has the plates to fix them. He doesn't have a chassis that can stand out in it.
>
> Brandt says the row's quieter with them all running again. He means that as a good thing.

Objectives are one plain instruction.

> Find the field radio in the chest and use it.
>
> Put the uplink card on the chassis, then on the dive chair.
>
> Carry the meal to Sorensen before it goes cold.

## Terminals and books

Written text can run longer than speech, but it's still one person writing to another.
No bullet lists of nouns. No headings inside a personal log.

> Day 18. Marsh came in on the supply run with a clipboard and no chassis. He says the company wants a number a day from us. I told him the company can walk over here and count them itself.

## Before a line ships

Read it out loud. If it sounds like something a person would say on a radio to someone
they like, it's done. If it sounds like a label, add the verbs back.

## Where the words live and who says them

- Every player-facing line is a key in `src/main/resources/assets/surrogate/lang/en_us.json`.
  Cinematic lines are `cinematic.surrogate.<scene>.<key>`. Errand text is `errand.surrogate.<name>.<key>`.
  Survivor radio is `survivor.surrogate.<name>.<key>`.
- The speaker is not in the lang file. It comes from the Java scene script that plays the line.
  Transit.java, Prologue.java and Housewarming.java call `say(H, "key")`, `radio(M, "key")`,
  `intercom(...)`, `system("...", "key")`. The letter is the crew member, so `radio(H, "call1")`
  means Halloran says `cinematic.surrogate.prologue.call1` over the radio.
- Keys the scripts don't name a speaker for, such as survivor radio and the television, are
  mapped by prefix in the `speakers` block of `tools/voices.json`.
- To see every line with its speaker and delivery in one list:

```bash
python tools/gen_voices.py --lines
```

## Voice lines

Recording uses ElevenLabs through `tools/gen_voices.py`. The key is the `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`
environment variable. Casting is in `tools/voices.json`. The account has 40,000 characters a
month and a full render is about 17,000, so don't record lines that are still being rewritten.

To find out how many lines exist and how many have a recording, count from the lang file, not
from the scripts:

```bash
python tools/gen_voices.py --missing
```

That compares every spoken lang key against the ogg files on disk and prints the totals, the
missing count per scene, and which missing lines have no known speaker. On 2026-09-08 that was
631 spoken keys, 218 recordings, 420 missing. Some crew and errand keys in that list are name
labels or narrator text and may never need a voice.

Don't trust `--lines` or `--render` for the total. They only see scenes listed in `SCRIPTS` at
the top of gen_voices.py, currently transit, prologue and housewarming, plus the prefixes in the
`speakers` block of voices.json. Flashback, assay, research, conference and most survivors are
not covered yet. To make a scene recordable, add its Java file and lang prefix to `SCRIPTS`, or
add a prefix to `speakers`, then check it appears in `--lines`.

Once a scene is covered, the dry run shows what recording would cost without billing anything:

```bash
python tools/gen_voices.py --render --dry
```

That lists every attributable line that has no recording or whose text, voice or settings
changed since it was recorded. To record them:

```bash
python tools/gen_voices.py --render
```

Then register the new files in sounds.json:

```bash
python tools/gen_sounds.py
```

Recordings go to `assets/surrogate/sounds/voice/<key>.ogg`.

## Keeping text and voice in step

`sounds/voice/manifest.json` stores a fingerprint of the exact text, voice id, model and settings
each recording was made from. `--render` compares that fingerprint to the current lang entry and
re-records anything that no longer matches. So the rule is simple:

1. Edit the line in en_us.json.
2. Run the dry render. Any line you changed shows up as stale.
3. Record once the text is final. Never assume a voice file is current because it exists.

A changed line with an old recording is a bug. Commit the lang change and the new ogg together.
